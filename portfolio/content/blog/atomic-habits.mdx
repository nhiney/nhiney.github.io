<EditorialArticle>

This book did not only teach me how to do things better. It made me look at myself more deeply. For me, *Atomic Habits* made one idea very clear: lasting change has to begin with identity. "What kind of person do I want to become?"

What I want to keep from *Atomic Habits* is not the pressure to change quickly. It is a gentler way of seeing change: every small action is helping me answer the question of who I believe I am.

<EditorialQuoteBox>
  "Tiny habits do not only change what we do. They change the person we believe we are."
</EditorialQuoteBox>

## Three levels of change

The book helped me see change through three levels: **outcomes** are "what I get," **processes** are "what I do each day," and **identity** is "who I want to become."

<ol className="atomic-steps">
  <li><strong>Outcomes</strong> -> What I get.</li>
  <li><strong>Processes</strong> -> What I do each day.</li>
  <li><strong>Identity</strong> -> Who I want to become.</li>
</ol>

For me, the most important point sits at the identity level. When I only focus on outcomes, change can feel like a destination far away. But when I return to the question "What kind of person do I want to become?", the small action in front of me feels closer, more honest, and more possible.

<EditorialHighlightBox title="Identity votes">
  <p>A small repeated action is a quiet vote for the person I am becoming.</p>
  <ul>
    <li>Writing every day -> A consistent person.</li>
    <li>Choosing healthier food -> A person who cares for herself.</li>
  </ul>
</EditorialHighlightBox>

## One small place to begin

I like this way of understanding habits because it does not force me to become a perfect version of myself overnight. It simply asks me to notice the small choices I repeat every day.

If I want change to last, maybe the first question is not only "What do I want to achieve?" but also "What kind of person am I quietly voting for?"

<div className="atomic-final-note">

For me, that is the part of *Atomic Habits* worth keeping: change does not always begin with a dramatic breakthrough. Sometimes it begins with one small action, repeated steadily enough that I slowly learn to believe in a new version of myself.

</div>
