Why I Should Stop Searching for Passion

Maybe the problem is not that I have not found my passion, but that I am waiting for a feeling too beautiful before beginning something very real.

Nguyen Thi Yen Nhi

June 27, 2026/5 min read/—views

There was a time when every time I opened YouTube, people seemed to be talking about passion.

Find your passion. Follow your passion. Live with passion. As if somewhere out there was a box tied with a ribbon, with my name on it, and if I waited patiently enough, opening it would make my whole life light up.

Meanwhile, I sat in front of my laptop between half-finished study tabs, an unfinished design file, a few saved courses from last month, and felt like someone lost in a supermarket. Everything looked interesting. Everything seemed worth trying. But nothing made my heart beat wildly the way it did in those speeches.

So I started wondering: maybe I am the kind of person who has no passion.

The question sounds dramatic, but it really did weigh on me. In this time, not having a passion can feel almost like a character flaw. You do not only need to study, work, and live decently. You also have to burn. You have to have something that makes your eyes light up when you talk about it. You have to have a beautiful answer when someone asks, "What are you passionate about?"

I hated that question for a long time.

Passion sounds beautiful, but it can also be heavy

The problem with the word "passion" is that it is often told after the story has already succeeded.

A famous designer says they loved shapes as a child. A great programmer says they fell in love with code at twelve. A founder says they were born to solve exactly this problem. After hearing that, I look at my own history and see that it has no dramatic soundtrack.

I did not come to technology through a lightning strike. I came through messier things: curiosity, pressure to find a career, a little desire to prove myself, a little enjoyment in making something run. Some days I like it. Some days I am bored. Some days I only work because the deadline is breathing down my neck.

If intense emotion is the proof of passion, I probably fail before the starting line.

But the more I work, the more I think I may have misunderstood the order. I thought I had to find passion first and then go deep. In reality, sometimes I have to do something deeply, long enough and honestly enough, before I begin to care for it.

Affection for a craft does not fall from the sky like rain. It is more like a relationship. At first there is only curiosity. Then familiarity. Then annoyance. Then wanting to quit. Then one day, quietly, I realize I have been attached for a while.

Expecting to love something on the first day may be unfair.

Curiosity is humbler, but more real

I like the word "curiosity" more than "passion."

Curiosity does not force me to burn. It only asks softly: can we try? Can we read a little more? Can we make one bad version first? Can we ask why that person did it that way?

Curiosity is lighter, so it is easier to carry. Passion often makes me feel as if I must choose one correct thing for my whole life. Curiosity lets me take one small step. Today, try writing a component. Tomorrow, read about UX. Next week, fix a backend bug. Not to immediately discover destiny, but to collect data about myself.

I realized I do not need a huge fire to begin. Sometimes I only need one match: "Wait, how does this work?"

And there is a blunt but necessary truth: getting better also creates love. When I am too bad at something, that thing is rarely charming. Everything feels irritating. Everything makes me feel stupid.

But after I pass the first clumsy stage, when my hands begin to feel the shape of the work, when I can do a small thing I could not do yesterday, the work becomes more attractive. Not because a sacred passion appeared. Because ability makes the feeling less hopeless.

A more practical way forward is to set a small experiment. Thirty days of writing. Two weeks learning one concept. One weekend building a rough draft. Then ask: does this make me more curious or more drained? Do I hate the whole thing, or only the early clumsiness?

Those answers are much more honest than sitting around waiting for a call from the universe.

Reminder to myself

Do not wait for a feeling big enough to begin. Choose something that makes me curious enough, then treat it seriously long enough for it to answer.

I do not ask myself "what is my passion?" as much as before. That question is too large and can freeze me. I ask smaller questions: what makes me want to understand more even when it is hard? In which field am I willing to be bad for a while? If I did not need to show anyone, what would I still want to learn?

Those questions are not glamorous. But they move me forward.

Maybe one day, looking back, I will call something passion. But if I do, it will not be a gift that fell from the sky. It will be something grown through many ordinary evenings, many wrong attempts, and many boring stretches that I did not abandon.

So if you have not found your passion, maybe you are not broken. Maybe you are only standing in front of too many billboards telling you life has to begin with lightning.

Ignore the lightning for a while. Choose something that makes you curious. Care for it long enough.

Maybe love does not arrive like fireworks. Maybe it arrives like a very slow tree, and one day you look back and realize it has shade.
