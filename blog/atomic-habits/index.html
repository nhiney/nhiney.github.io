Atomic Habits — changing habits starts with who we believe we are

A book that did more than suggest better habits: it made me look more honestly at who I am becoming.

Nguyễn Thị Yến Nhi

July 3, 2026/2 min read/—views

This book did more than show me ways to build better habits. It made me look more honestly at who I am becoming. The idea I keep returning to is identity: "What kind of person do I want to become?"

What I want to keep from Atomic Habits is not the pressure to change quickly. It is a gentler way of seeing change: every small action is helping me answer the question of who I believe I am.

My paraphrase of James Clear's idea: tiny habits do more than change what I do; they give me evidence about the person I believe I am becoming.

Three levels of change

The book helped me see change through three levels: outcomes are "what I get," processes are "what I do each day," and identity is "who I want to become."

  1. Outcomes -> What I get.
  2. Processes -> What I do each day.
  3. Identity -> Who I want to become.

For me, the most important point sits at the identity level. When I only focus on outcomes, change can feel like a destination far away. But when I return to that identity question, I can see the small action in front of me as something closer, more honest, and more possible.

Identity votes

A small repeated action is a quiet vote for the person I am becoming.

  • Writing every day -> A consistent person.
  • Choosing healthier food -> A person who cares for herself.

One small place to begin

I like this way of understanding habits because it does not force me to become a perfect version of myself overnight. It simply asks me to notice the small choices I repeat every day.

If I want change to last, maybe the first question is not only "What do I want to achieve?" but also "What kind of person am I quietly voting for?"

For me, that is the part of Atomic Habits worth keeping: change does not always begin with a dramatic breakthrough. Sometimes it begins with one small action, repeated steadily enough that I slowly learn to believe in a new version of myself.
